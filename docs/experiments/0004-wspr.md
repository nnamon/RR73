# Experiment 0004: a WSPR decoder

Status: accepted 2026-10-14 (preset `wspr`). Measured on branch `feat/wspr-core`; the commits are named in each section.

## Question

Can RR73 decode WSPR with its own code down to the protocol's nominal threshold of about -28 dB SNR in 2500 Hz, with no false decodes on noise, in a time per 120 s slot that suits an iPhone, and how does it compare with WSJT-X's `wsprd` on the same slots?

## Protocol

From K1JT's descriptions and G4JNT's "The WSPR Coding Process"; the codec is checked bit for bit against WSJT-X's `wsprsim` (`fixtures/wspr/wsjtx.json`, 314 messages).

- 50-bit message: a 28-bit callsign field and 15 bits of locator plus 7 of power (types 1 to 3, `crates/rr73-wspr/src/message.rs`).
- K = 32, rate 1/2 convolutional code with a 31-bit zero tail: 162 code bits, bit-reversal interleaved.
- 162 4-FSK symbols, `sync + 2 × data`, 8192 samples at 12 kHz (0.683 s), 1.4648 Hz apart, phase-continuous: 110.6 s starting 1 s into an even UTC minute, centred within 1400 to 1600 Hz of audio.
- No CRC. A decoder can only check that the decoded bits form a valid message.

## Design

Each stage is a trait with named implementations in `crates/rr73-decode/src/wspr`, as in the FT8 pipeline (`docs/architecture.md`).

1. Downconversion: one FFT of the slot (1 440 000 points), cut to 375 Hz around 1500 Hz with a raised-cosine taper beyond ±150 Hz, and one inverse FFT of 45 000 points. A symbol is then 256 complex samples and a tone spacing one cycle per symbol.
2. Spectrogram: one-symbol rectangular window, zero-padded to 2 symbols (0.73 Hz bins), quarter-symbol hop, each bin divided by its median over time so powers read in units of noise.
3. Search: for every tone-0 bin, start (quarter-symbol steps, DT -2 to 6 s) and drift (±4 Hz over the transmission in 0.5 Hz steps), the sum over symbols of `±((P1 + P3) - (P0 + P2))` with the sync bit's sign. On noise this sum has standard deviation `sqrt(4 × 162)`, so the score reads in standard deviations. Candidates need 4 or more and are ranked by the sum as a fraction of the power read at the same cells, which puts a weak signal on its sync pattern above a strong one read at the wrong time or frequency; at most 60 local maxima.
4. Refinement (`coherent`): a coarse power-based search for start, frequency and drift; then frequency and drift from phase coherence. With a modulation index of 1 every tone advances the phase by `2 pi f0 T` per symbol modulo whole cycles, so after rotating each symbol's correlations by the running sum of `2 pi f0 T` the sent tones share one phase whatever the data. The metric is the energy of coherent sums over blocks of 5 symbols, each block taking its best data pattern. Start time is then searched with the same metric in 4-sample and 1-sample steps (10.7 and 2.7 ms), and frequency and drift once more.
5. Soft bits (`block`): for each symbol, every data pattern of the 7 symbols centred on it is scored with the Rician likelihood `ln I0(2 A |sum| / N)` of the coherent sum of its phase-aligned correlations; the bit's log-likelihood ratio is the log-sum over patterns with the bit set minus the log-sum over those with it clear. `N` comes from the two tones each sync bit rules out, `A²` from the two it allows. With 1 symbol this is ordinary noncoherent binary FSK. When the Fano decoder finds no valid message from 7-symbol blocks, it tries again with single symbols: real signals lose phase coherence that synthetic ones keep (see Recordings).
6. Fano decoder: textbook algorithm (Lin and Costello), metric `log2(2 P(bit)) - bias` per code bit, threshold step 1 bit, bias 0.4, at most 10 000 steps per input bit; the tail allows only zeros.
7. Validation: the 50 bits must unpack (callsign field in range, valid locator and power for the type), a type 1 or 2 callsign must pack back to the same field (digit in position 2 or 3, letters after it, a letter before it), and the SNR estimate must be at least -40 dB.
8. Passes: up to 2. Each decode's symbols are subtracted from the baseband (per symbol, its projection on the sent tone), and the search runs again. Within a pass, candidates within 4.5 Hz of a decode of that pass are skipped as its sidelobes; in the next pass, candidates within 2 Hz and 1 s of a subtracted decode are skipped as its residue. A repeated text is dropped.

SNR is the mean power of the sent tones less the noise, over the noise per correlation, converted to 2500 Hz: the correlation integrates one symbol, so its noise bandwidth is 1.4648 Hz and the offset is `-10 log10(2500 / 1.4648) = -32.32 dB`.

Rejected on the way, with numbers below: single-symbol soft bits alone (the first version, 2.6 dB worse at SNR50 on synthetic slots), 7-symbol blocks alone (10 of 32 on the recordings), power-only refinement (start errors up to 0.1 s cost 1 to 2 dB once soft bits use phase), a lower sync threshold (3.5: one more decode in 600 signals for 3 to 4 times the time per slot), more candidates or a third pass (one more decode in 400 busy-window signals each).

## Method

- Sets from `rr73-bench synth` (`docs/bench.md`): `wspr-snr-sweep` (one type 1 signal per slot from -34 to -20 dB in 1 dB steps; `awgn` and `fading` with Watterson 0.1 Hz spread, 1 ms), `wspr-noise`, `wspr-smoke`, `wspr-band` (20 signals per slot, 8 Hz apart, -28 to -10 dB, drift up to ±0.01 Hz/s). Large sets are rendered in memory with `rr73-bench run --synth`; their results equal those from the written files (checked on the 300-slot sweep: identical decodes).
- `wsprd` from WSJT-X 2.7.0-rc3 (Ubuntu package, `tools/wsjtx`), run by `tools/wsjtx/wspr_reference.py` with `-f 14.0956` and default options, and again with `-d`, in a fresh directory per slot. Its decodes are scored with `rr73-bench import`. `wsprd` reports frequency as the signal centre to 1 Hz and DT to 0.1 s, from 1 s into the slot, as RR73 does.
- Stage-by-stage losses with `crates/rr73-decode/examples/wspr_probe.rs`, which decodes each slot at the found candidate and at the true position.
- Docker Desktop on the aarch64 dev machine (Apple M4, 10 CPUs), release build. Other workloads ran on the host throughout; the load average is given with each timing.

## Results

### Codec

All 314 messages of `fixtures/wspr/wsjtx.json` (236 type 1, 47 type 2, 31 type 3) pack to the same 11 bytes and 162 channel symbols as `wsprsim`, and unpack to the text `wsprcode` shows, except one locator `wsprcode` blanks (`crates/rr73-wspr/tests/wsjtx.rs`).

### Recordings

10 WSPR slots recorded with `rr73-kiwi` from the SARTS Web-888 in Singapore on 2026-10-13 (NTP timing): 20m from 17:50 to 17:58 UTC and 40m from 18:08 to 18:16 UTC, local night. `wsprd` 2.7.0-rc3 decoded 32 messages (12 on 20m, 20 on 40m), the reference for recall.

| Decoder | Matched | Not in the wsprd list |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wspr`, 7-symbol blocks only | 10 | 1 |
| `wspr`, single symbols only | 26 | 1 |
| `wspr`, 7-symbol blocks then single symbols, DT to 4 s | 28 | 2 |
| `wspr` (as above, DT to 6 s) | 29 | 2 |
| `wspr-deep` | 28 | 3 |

Long blocks lost two thirds of the real signals that synthetic slots said they would gain: the recorded signals keep their phase over fewer symbols than the Watterson model at 0.1 Hz does. The fallback to single symbols recovers them at no loss on synthetic sets. The DT range grew to 6 s because `wsprd` decoded a station at DT 5.4 s.

The two decodes outside the `wsprd` list are `KE7ZXJ CN95 33` at -30 dB (20m, 17:54) and `EB1A IN53 27` at -28 dB (20m, 17:56). Both stations transmitted on 20m in those slots by the WSPRnet spot database (`wspr.live`: 57 and 212 spots of other receivers), and both callsigns match their locators (W7 area in CN95, Spain in IN53). `wspr-deep` adds `ZS1LCD JF95 30` at 18:08, a station `wsprd` decoded at the same frequency in the next two slots. Missed: `GI3VAF IO74 37` (-27 dB) and `G4MRS JO02 43` (-24 dB, DT -1.4 s) at 17:52, and `DU4MD PK13 23` (-23 dB, DT 2.6 s) at 18:16.

On the 29 shared decodes the `wspr` SNR reads a median 3.3 dB below `wsprd`'s. On synthetic slots the `wspr` estimate matches the truth and `wsprd` reads 0.1 to 0.4 dB high, so the gap comes from the recordings: most likely real signals spread power outside the one-symbol tone bins the estimate reads. Unverified.

## Gate

CI gates `wspr` on `wspr-smoke` and 50 `wspr-noise` slots rendered in memory (`bench/wspr.json`, `bench/gate-wspr.toml`): recall may drop by one signal of 51 between architectures, false decodes may not rise.

## Open risks

- 10 recorded slots from one receiver at night. Daytime 20m with 30 or more signals in the window, and receivers with other noise, are not measured.
- The ITU-R moderate profile (0.5 Hz spread) was not swept for WSPR; the fading sweep uses 0.1 Hz.
- The SNR scale on real signals differs from `wsprd` by about 3 dB (above).
- Types 2 and 3 decode on synthetic slots and resolve hashes across slots in tests. From the air, both decoders decoded one of each: `VK3ARW/2 33` and `<...> QE38ON 33` (-27 dB), the latter without its callsign because the station's full callsign was not sent in these slots.
