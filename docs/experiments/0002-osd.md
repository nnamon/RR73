# Experiment 0002: ordered-statistics decoding after belief propagation

Status: hypothesis recorded 2026-10-13, before implementation.

## Question

When belief propagation fails on a candidate, does ordered-statistics decoding (OSD) recover enough real messages to lower SNR50 and raise recall on recorded bands, without false decodes and within 300 ms per slot single-threaded?

## Why it should help, and why it is risky

Sum-product belief propagation with 25 iterations stops short of what the (174,91) code can correct: on short codes the gap to maximum-likelihood decoding is commonly 1 to 2 dB. OSD closes part of it. It sorts the code bits by reliability, takes the 91 most reliable independent positions as an information set, re-encodes their hard decisions (order 0), and tries flipping 1 or 2 of the least reliable of them (orders 1 and 2), keeping the candidate closest to the received LLRs.

OSD always returns a codeword, so every attempt can turn into a decode. With order 2 over a window of 40 positions it tests 821 codewords per failed candidate, and the 14-bit CRC lets 1 in 16384 random codewords through, so a busy slot (about 400 failed candidates over 3 passes) would see about 20 random CRC passes. Acceptance therefore needs more than the CRC:

- Distance: the candidate's hard errors against the channel decisions, and its reliability-weighted distance, must stay below thresholds set on noise.
- Message plausibility, for OSD decodes only: a standard message (i3 1 or 2) with both callsigns sent in full and plausible (no Q prefix, no prefix starting with 0 or 1); optionally at least one callsign already known from other decodes.

## Method

- `LdpcDecoder` implementation `bp-osd`: sum-product as in `baseline`, then OSD on failure. Options: order (0, 1, 2), window of flipped positions, reliabilities from the channel LLRs or from the last BP state, and the distance thresholds.
- The validator applies the plausibility checks to OSD decodes.
- Presets `subtract-3-osd1` and `subtract-3-osd2` on top of `subtract-3`, which stays unchanged.
- False decodes measured on 5000 noise-only slots, on every synthetic set, and as a stress test on the same noise with the sync threshold lowered so that every slot sends about 140 candidates to OSD. Unmatched decodes on recordings are checked against the callsigns in the reference lists.
- The SARTS corpus holds 57 slots when this is written: 26 on 40 m with `jt9 -d 3` references and 31 on 15 m without (a background recording is adding more). The 15 m slots get `jt9 -d 3` references from the `wsjtx` tool before measuring, and the results state the slot count used.

## Expected effect, against `subtract-3`

| Measure | `subtract-3` | Expected with OSD order 1 to 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SNR50, AWGN | -18.85 dB | -19.3 to -19.8 dB |
| SNR50, Watterson 0.5 Hz 1 ms | -15.17 dB | -15.4 to -15.9 dB |
| SARTS recall (40 m set) | 0.587 | +0.01 to +0.03 |
| ft8_lib public recall | 0.876 | +0.005 to +0.02 |
| False decodes, 5000 noise slots and synthetic sets | 0 | 0 at the chosen thresholds |
| Time per slot, single-threaded, busiest slot | 69 ms | under 200 ms |

The gain on recordings is smaller than in AWGN because most missed signals there are missed by the search or masked, which OSD does not change.

## Acceptance

Keep a preset only if SNR50 or recall on the recorded sets improves, false decodes stay at 0 on all synthetic and noise sets (including 5000 noise slots), unmatched decodes on recordings do not rise beyond those whose callsigns appear in the reference lists, and the slowest slot stays under 300 ms single-threaded native. Otherwise record the result here as negative.
