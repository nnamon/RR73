# Experiment 0002: ordered-statistics decoding after belief propagation

Status: accepted 2026-10-13 (`subtract-3-osd2` proposed as the next shipped preset). Hypothesis recorded before implementation (commit bd691ea); results measured on the tree of commit cecce1f.

## Question

When belief propagation fails on a candidate, does ordered-statistics decoding (OSD) recover enough real messages to lower SNR50 and raise recall on recorded bands, without false decodes and within 300 ms per slot single-threaded?

## Why it should help, and why it is risky

Sum-product belief propagation with 25 iterations stops short of what the (174,91) code can correct: on short codes the gap to maximum-likelihood decoding is commonly 1 to 2 dB. OSD closes part of it. It sorts the code bits by reliability, takes the 91 most reliable independent positions as an information set, re-encodes their hard decisions (order 0), and tries flipping 1 or 2 of the least reliable of them (orders 1 and 2), keeping the candidate closest to the received LLRs.

OSD always returns a codeword, so every attempt can turn into a decode. With order 2 over a window of 40 positions it tests 821 codewords per failed candidate, and the 14-bit CRC lets 1 in 16384 random codewords through, so a busy slot (about 400 failed candidates over 3 passes) would see about 20 random CRC passes. Acceptance therefore needs more than the CRC:

- Distance: the candidate's hard errors against the channel decisions, and its reliability-weighted distance, must stay below thresholds set on noise.
- Message plausibility, for OSD decodes only: a standard message (i3 1 or 2) with both callsigns sent in full and plausible (no Q prefix, no prefix starting with 0 or 1); optionally at least one callsign already known from other decodes.

## Method

- `LdpcDecoder` implementation `bp-osd`: sum-product as in `baseline`, then OSD on failure. Options: order (0, 1, 2), window of flipped positions, reliabilities from the channel LLRs or from the last BP state, and the distance thresholds.
- The validator applies the plausibility checks to OSD decodes.
- Presets `subtract-3-osd1` and `subtract-3-osd2` on top of `subtract-3`, which stays unchanged.
- False decodes measured on 5000 noise-only slots, on every synthetic set, and as a stress test on the same noise with the sync threshold lowered so that every slot sends about 140 candidates to OSD. Unmatched decodes on recordings are checked against the callsigns in the reference lists.
- The SARTS corpus holds 57 slots when this is written: 26 on 40 m with `jt9 -d 3` references and 31 on 15 m without (a background recording is adding more). The 15 m slots get `jt9 -d 3` references from the `wsjtx` tool before measuring, and the results state the slot count used.

## Expected effect, against `subtract-3`

| Measure | `subtract-3` | Expected with OSD order 1 to 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SNR50, AWGN | -18.85 dB | -19.3 to -19.8 dB |
| SNR50, Watterson 0.5 Hz 1 ms | -15.17 dB | -15.4 to -15.9 dB |
| SARTS recall (40 m set) | 0.587 | +0.01 to +0.03 |
| ft8_lib public recall | 0.876 | +0.005 to +0.02 |
| False decodes, 5000 noise slots and synthetic sets | 0 | 0 at the chosen thresholds |
| Time per slot, single-threaded, busiest slot | 69 ms | under 200 ms |

The gain on recordings is smaller than in AWGN because most missed signals there are missed by the search or masked, which OSD does not change.

## Acceptance

Keep a preset only if SNR50 or recall on the recorded sets improves, false decodes stay at 0 on all synthetic and noise sets (including 5000 noise slots), unmatched decodes on recordings do not rise beyond those whose callsigns appear in the reference lists, and the slowest slot stays under 300 ms single-threaded native. Otherwise record the result here as negative.

## Results

Measured with `rr73-bench run` and `compare` in the `rust` container on the Apple M4 (Docker Desktop, 10 CPUs), release build, with other workloads on the host (load average 8 to 9). Sets: the 60 ft8_lib public files against their WSJT-X lists; 73 SARTS slots (26 on 40 m from 21:49 to 22:01 UTC and 47 on 15 m from 23:17 to 23:31 UTC on 2026-10-12, 1981 `jt9 2.7.0-rc3 -8 -d 3` decodes; the 47 15 m slots got their references from `reference.py` before measuring, and the recording that continued afterwards was left out); `snr-sweep` (840 slots each, AWGN and fading); `crowded`; `smoke`; `noise-ci`; `noise` with 5000 slots.

### Setting the limits

Permissive runs (no distance or hard-error limit) show how far OSD's real and false codewords lie from the received LLRs. On the sweep, OSD codewords that match the truth have 15 to 35 hard errors (median 26) and a weighted distance of 0.024 to 0.082 (median 0.053); false ones have 28 to 57 hard errors (median 43) and 0.057 to 0.158 (median 0.10). The estimated SNR separates them further: every real OSD decode on all sets reads -23.7 dB or more, while 95 % of the false ones read below -24.6 dB.

The limits (at most 30 hard errors, distance at most 0.07, SNR at least -24 dB, plausible standard message) were set on the sweep and on a stress test over noise slots 0 to 2499: the sync threshold lowered to 0 dB so every slot sends 140 candidates to OSD. Within that tuning data the nearest false codeword that passes the SNR and message checks has 33 hard errors. Noise slots 2500 to 4999 were held out for the check below.

Choices measured on the way:

| Variant (permissive limits, standard messages) | Real OSD decodes on the sweep | At 0 false on sweep and tuning stress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order 0 | 10 | 10 |
| Order 1, all 91 positions, channel LLRs | 30 | 25 |
| Order 2, window 40, channel LLRs | 39 | 26 |
| Order 2, window 40, BP state | 56 | 26 |
| Order 2, window 91, BP state | 66 | not measured (1127 false before limits) |

The BP state finds more real codewords but also more false ones close to the received LLRs, so at zero false decodes it gains nothing over the channel LLRs. `standard-known` (a callsign already decoded) removes every OSD decode in single-slot runs and was dropped; it may suit the app, which keeps callsigns across slots.

`compare subtract-3 subtract-3-osd2` over all 7123 slots: 134 decodes gained (124 matched, 10 unmatched), none lost. All 10 unmatched OSD decodes on the recordings carry a callsign that appears in some reference list (3 have the same text as a reference decode in another slot), so they look like real stations missing from those lists. Over both stress halves `subtract-3-osd2` produced 28 OSD codewords within the distance limits; the message and SNR checks rejected 12 and the rest repeated a rejected codeword, so none became a decode. On the SARTS slots 352 OSD codewords passed the limits, 83 became new decodes, 18 were rejected as implausible and the rest repeated a decode.

With 0 false decodes in 700000 stress attempts, the rate per attempt is below 4.3 per million (95 % upper bound). A busy SARTS slot makes about 260 attempts, so the bound is about 1 false decode in 900 busy slots, or 0.27 per hour of continuous decoding.

Against the hypothesis: SNR50 in AWGN improved by 0.17 dB, short of the expected 0.45 to 0.95 dB; with fading by 0.50 dB, inside the expected range. Recall on the 40 m SARTS slots rose by 0.047, above the expected 0.01 to 0.03; on the public files by 0.018, inside the range. The AWGN gain is small because near SNR50 the demodulator's LLRs carry more hard errors than the limits allow; the real OSD decodes that the limits remove are mostly there.

### Callsign rule

The plausibility check on OSD messages first rejected callsigns starting with Q, 0 or 1. The ITU allocates no call sign series starting with Q (the Q codes), 0 or 1 (Radio Regulations, Appendix 42). Two DXCC entities still use an unallocated 1 prefix on the air: 1A, the Sovereign Military Order of Malta (1A0KM), and 1S, the Spratly Islands. The rule now accepts 1A and 1S and keeps rejecting:

- Q: never an amateur prefix.
- 0: no allocation and no DXCC entity; the 0 calls heard on the air are unofficial (for example 0S for Seborga).
- 1 followed by any other letter: no allocation and no DXCC entity. 1B (Northern Cyprus) is the one seen regularly; it is not a DXCC entity, and its stations stay decodable whenever belief propagation succeeds, since the rule applies to OSD codewords only.

None of the 4210 SARTS and 1298 public reference decodes has a callsign with these prefixes (the only 1 prefix in them, 1Y8QSJ, sits in a DXpedition message that OSD never accepts). Measured again with the change: 0 false decodes on 5000 noise slots, 0 in both stress halves (700000 attempts), 0 on every synthetic set; recall unchanged on every set (SARTS 3113 of 4210, public 1160 of 1298, sweep 461 and 327 of 840).

### FT4 floor (2026-10-15)

The limits above were set on FT8. On the `ft4-sweep` set (experiment 0006), 10000 FT4 noise slots gave 2 OSD decodes, `BL7CXS 9C5UQI/P PK50` at -22.1 dB and `N81ETB WL4CVB R GM61` at -20.2 dB, and the FT4 `late-start` subsets 6 more, at -21.1 to -22.0 dB. True FT4 decodes report -16.5 dB at the lowest (28 OSD and 720 belief propagation decodes on `ft4-sweep`, 141 and 4836 on `late-start`): FT4 needs more SNR than FT8, and the FT8 floor of -24 dB leaves room for noise. An FT4 floor of -20, -19 or -18 dB (`validate.osd_ft4_min_snr_db`) removes all 8 and loses no true decode on either set. The OSD presets take -19 dB, 2.5 dB under the weakest true decode and 1.2 dB above the strongest false one. FT8 decodes do not change.

## Decision

Accept `subtract-3-osd2` and propose it as the next shipped preset. It raises recall on both recorded sets (+0.036 on the 73 SARTS slots and +0.037 on all 350, +0.018 on the public files) and SNR50 (0.17 dB in AWGN, 0.50 dB with fading), keeps false decodes at 0 on 5000 noise slots, every synthetic set and 700000 stress attempts, and its slowest slot takes 129 ms single-threaded, under the 300 ms budget. `subtract-3-osd1` keeps about 60 % of the gain at 70 % of the extra time and stays available.

`bench/subtract-3.json` and the CI gate are unchanged until the preset change is agreed; the app's preset is not changed here.

## Open risks

- One matched decode was lost on the 350 SARTS slots because extra decodes changed what the last pass sees; with only 3 passes, subtraction order matters at the margin.
- The limits were set on AWGN noise. Real band noise (impulses, birdies, other modes) may produce OSD codewords with other statistics; the unmatched decodes on recordings are the only check so far, and all looked real.
- The false-decode bound rests on 5000 noise slots; 0 false in 700000 attempts bounds the rate, it does not show it is zero.
- Rejecting callsigns that start with Q, 0 or 1 loses real special-event stations that use them; none appear in the reference lists.
- Timing comes from a loaded host; the per-slot best of 5 is the best estimate of the decoder's own cost.
